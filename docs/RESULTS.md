# Reading the results

All numbers live in [results/tables.md](../results/tables.md) (rendered) and
[results/results.json](../results/results.json) (raw). Both are generated by
`python scripts/reproduce.py`; nothing here restates them. This page explains what the columns
mean and how the ID and OOD numbers should be compared.

## Splits

- **ID test:** a 60/20/20 random split stratified by task family, duplicate prompts kept together.
  Models are fit on train, thresholds are calibrated on val and frozen, numbers are reported on test.
- **OOD (LOFO):** leave-one-task-family-out. For each family, the model and its threshold are built
  without that family and evaluated on it; predictions are pooled over the same test prompts as the
  ID rows, so the two are directly comparable.

## Metrics

- **Deployed operating point:** quality retained and cost saving relative to always-strong, at a
  threshold chosen on validation to hit 95% or 90% of strong quality. This is the headline.
- **Lift vs random:** quality points above random routing at the same realised cost. This is the
  number that separates "routes well" from "just moves along the cost-quality line". Zero means no
  better than random; the CI tells you whether the sign is resolved.
- **APGR, AUC, CPT:** whole-curve frontier metrics from sweeping the strong fraction on test. APGR
  counts calls, not dollars (random = 0.5), so a router can score well on it while adding nothing
  at matched cost.
- **"test sweep" columns:** thresholds picked on the test curve itself, hence optimistic; use the
  deployed operating points for claims.
- **Within-family APGR:** ranks prompts inside a single task family only, so it measures difficulty
  prediction instead of dataset identification (random = 0.5).
- **ECE:** calibration error of the plain classifier's P(needs strong).

## Where the evidence is thin

- Families with few test prompts (mbpp 85, mtbench 16, chinese 157) have wide uncertainty and are
  excluded from the within-family summary.
- Overhead figures come from one CPU, in-process.
- Everything is RouterBench's automatic scoring; see the "Not validated" section of the README.
